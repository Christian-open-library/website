Corrections log

Errors found and fixed in the books, the connector and the library's public pages, newest first.

To report one, write to hello@christianopenlibrary.org.

16 November 2026

The connector

  • Some quotation cards began with stray marks. The checker's card shows the words from the book, and a mark from the text just before them could come first: "* grace is but glory begun" (an italics mark), or part of the sentence before. The quotation now starts with its own words. In place from 16 November.
  • Some present-day writers were taken for older ones. The quotation checker treats a name that matches no writer in the library as a possible misspelling, so "J. I. Packer" was read as Joseph Parker, "T. D. Jakes" as any writer named James, "Erwin Lutzer" as Luther, and "Mother Teresa of Calcutta" as Teresa of Avila. Words of one of those older writers credited to the present-day one could be reported as found in that writer's works. About 160 commonly quoted present-day Christian writers are now matched by their own names only. When words credited to one of them aren't in the library, the checker now says their books are still in copyright, instead of only "not in the library". In place from 16 November.
  • The website's quotation checker could show an answer from before a fix. It keeps each answer for a day so that a shared link opens quickly, and a kept answer survived changes to the checker. "Be killing sin or it will be killing you", credited to John Owen, was shown as found with different wording, though it is word for word in Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers. Kept answers are now dropped whenever the checker changes, and after every change the checker is tried on quotations whose right answers are known, this one among them. In place from 16 November.
  • Catechism answers were reported as worded differently. The first question and answer of the Westminster Shorter Catechism, quoted exactly, came back as worded differently, because the "A." that marks the answer was counted as a word left out. The marks of a question and an answer ("Q.", "A.", "Answ.") are no longer counted as words. In place from 16 November.
  • The quotation checker graded answers in a confusing way. "Jesus said, I am the bread of life" was called close to John 6:35 because the verse has "unto them" in between, and its share picture showed only the words that matched, so the exact verse appeared under the word "close". The checker no longer grades answers. It says where the words come from (a Bible verse, or the earliest book in the library that has them), shows that book's own wording, and says in one line when the wording differs from yours or when the name given is not the writer. A verse quoted in part, or in a translation the library does not hold, is now found as that verse. Words much reworded in another writer's book are no longer given as the source; they are listed under Details. In place from 16 November.

The books

  • The Paradise or Garden of the Holy Fathers was credited to Athanasius, and had no text. Its two files held only links to page images. The work is E. A. Wallis Budge's 1907 translation of a seventh-century Syriac collection of Athanasius's Life of Antony, Palladius, Jerome and the Sayings of the Fathers. The empty files are removed and Budge's full text is added under his name. In place from the rebuild on the night of 16 November.
  • Early Christian writings were missing in their original languages. The library had the Church Fathers in English only. Added 441 works in the original Greek, Latin and Syriac: the First1KGreek and Perseus Greek texts (including the Septuagint, the Westcott-Hort New Testament and Josephus), the Vienna Corpus Latin Fathers, Migne's Greek Fathers, Bede, Isidore, Aldhelm and Gildas, and the Digital Syriac Corpus. Editions after 1930 are left out. The Greek and Syriac scans and transcriptions have errors, so check wording against the source. In place from 16 November.

15 November 2026

The connector

  • Some Scripture citations were counted under the wrong book. A book number that does not exist, such as "3 Chron." (a scanning error for "2 Chron.") or "3 Cor.", was read as the first book, and the older Catholic names "4 Kings" and "IV Reg." were read as 1 Kings. Such numbers are now left out, "3 Kings", "4 Kings", "III Reg." and "IV Reg." count as 1 and 2 Kings, and "3 Esdras" and "4 Esdras" as 1 and 2 Esdras. About 2,100 citations move to the right book and about 500 wrong ones are dropped. Reported by a library user. In place from the rebuild on the night of 15 November.
  • The quotation checker missed some reworded quotations. A quotation of common words with a word or two changed could be reported as not found even when the library has it. "Do all the good you can, by all the means you can, in all the ways you can", credited to John Wesley, was not found, though D. L. Moody, Spurgeon and F. B. Meyer have it with two words different. The checker now also looks for short pieces of a quotation close together, and finds it.
  • The quotation checker was slow on quotations of very common words, and missed a few more drifted ones. Hudson Taylor's "God's work done in God's way will never lack God's supply" took the longest of any check, because the words "God's", "will" and "way" are in a third or more of the library. The checker now finds the passages that hold those words close together without ranking the whole library first, which makes that step about four times faster, and it compares 60 passages found by meaning instead of 40. On a new test of 300 slightly changed quotations taken from the library, it finds the right book for 284 (was 281), and it still says "not found" for all 50 lines that are not in the library.
  • The quotation checker gave some wrong answers that sounded sure. Tested on 156 quotations as people really post them, checked by hand against the library, it gave 9 answers that were wrong but sounded certain. Words credited to "Augustine of Hippo" or "M'Cheyne" were reported as "not by" them while the passage shown was from their own books, because those forms of their names matched no author in the library. Lines by present-day writers were reported as found in old books that share only a stock phrase with them, such as "the ultimate goal of the Church". D. L. Moody was credited with a book by his son, Paul D. Moody. And words a later writer gives as someone else's ("Well says Baxter, ...") were reported as "not by" Baxter. Now such names match the library's author, initials keep two writers with one surname apart, a passage must hold most of a quotation and not just part of it, and words found only where a later writer credits them to the named author are reported as "found only as quoted". On the same test it now gives 3 such answers instead of 9, and finds as much as before. In place from 15 November.
  • A check that ran out of time could say "not found". When the quotation checker ran short of time before it had searched, it could report the words as not in the library without saying the search was cut short. It now says so, and the website's checker asks you to try again instead of giving an answer.

Public pages

  • The home page said there were no modern books. Present-day church documents are in the connector by permission of their church bodies. It now says so. The home and download pages also gave the size of the library as about 6,400 works in 15,300 files; it is about 7,100 works in 16,600 files.

14 November 2026

The connector

  • "Found word for word" could be wrong in scanned books. The quotation checker reported a word-for-word match when one word differed by a single letter in a scanned book. Such a difference may be a misreading of the scan or a different word. It now says a word differs and to check the scan, and when both are ordinary words (not and now, sin and son) it says "worded differently".
  • Quotations joined by "..." were too easily "found". Pieces of only a word or two, scattered through a passage, counted as found word for word. They are now reported as found with words left out.

Public pages

  • The cost card planned for far more people than use it. From 12 November it costed the coming months as if hundreds of people a day used the connector, and didn't say so: December showed about $102. It now projects from actual use (December: about $42).
  • The security review's summary was out of date. It still said the connector only reads public-domain books and stores no accounts. It now says it also holds a few documents shown by permission of their church bodies, and that sign-in accounts are held by WorkOS. The reviews are now listed newest first.

13 November 2026

The books

  • Three works were removed or replaced for copyright reasons. One copy of Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress was a 1988 edition with a modern introduction; Bunyan's text stays, from Project Gutenberg and EEBO-TCP. Pascal's Memorial came with an unsigned modern introduction and translation, and was removed. The Confession of St. Patrick named no translator; it is replaced by an 1880 translation from Project Gutenberg, with the Letter to Coroticus.
  • Church family records corrected. Several dates and other names were fixed, for example the Bulgarian Exarchate (1870 and 1872).

11 November 2026

Public pages

  • "Exactly as printed" was wrong. The front page said passages were returned exactly as printed. They are returned exactly as they stand in the library's copy, and the copies have errors, most of all in scanned books. The page now says so.
  • Not every result has a year and page. The front page and the connector's description promised both. Some sources give neither. Both now say "where the source gives them".
  • "Stores no accounts" and "private by design" said too much. Sign-in accounts are held by WorkOS, and search words go to Voyage AI. The claims are replaced by a page naming everyone who sees a question.
  • The Voyage AI account is now opted out of data use. Until today it wasn't, so Voyage's terms allowed it to keep search words and use them to improve its models.
  • "No affiliation with any church or denomination" was reworded to what is meant: no church or denomination directs or endorses the project.
  • The security review is described more exactly. It was carried out with Claude, Anthropic's AI. It wasn't an outside audit.
  • Monergism's editions were removed. I asked Monergism for permission to keep 338 texts taken from its free ebooks. Monergism replied that its editions may not be placed in another library, database or AI system, so all 338 were removed from the library and the connector. A separate collection of Monergism's ebooks that had been shared with library members is no longer shared. The same works will be added again from other sources.
  • The first removal of Monergism's editions was incomplete. It took out each work's main file but left 342 "Part" files, which hold the text of the 98 longer works, in the library members can download. They were found and removed later the same day. They were not in the connector's catalogue.
  • Most of the removed works are back, from other sources. Of the 338 works, 126 were already in the library inside their authors' collected Works. Another 161 were added again from independent copies: EEBO-TCP, Project Gutenberg and Internet Archive scans. 51 are not back: 12 were Monergism's own compilations, and for 39 no usable independent copy was found.
  • The count of works was corrected to about 4,400 after the removal, then to about 4,600 once the restored works were added.
  • CCEL is now credited. CCEL confirmed that its texts may be quoted and used as long as its files are not sold.
  • Credit added for the Greek and Latin texts. They come from the Open Greek and Latin Project under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each file and the front page now give the credit, the license and a note of what was changed.

The connector

  • A missing book is now said plainly. Asking for a book the library doesn't have used to return loosely related titles with a small note at the end. The result now opens with "NOT FOUND" and tells the AI not to quote the book from memory.
  • Results now say how well they match. Each is marked exact, close, partial or loose. When nothing is close, the result opens with "NO CLOSE MATCH" or "NOT FOUND".
  • Scanned passages are now marked in search results. Before, only the book listing said a text was a scan.

The books

  • Berkhof's Systematic Theology was removed. It is a revised edition of his Reformed Dogmatics (1932), whose copyright was renewed in 1960, so much of its text is still protected in the United States until 1 January 2028. Other sites list it as public domain; that doesn't hold. It is out of the library and leaves the connector at its next update. Berkhof's Summary of Christian Doctrine (1938, never renewed) and Introduction to the New Testament (1915) stay.
  • Two more reprint years were corrected. Erasmus's In Praise of Folly: 1958 corrected to 1688, the year of John Wilson's translation in the file. Melanchthon's Apology of the Augsburg Confession: 1989 corrected to 1921, the Bente and Dau translation in the Triglot Concordia.
  • Four more works were removed. Teresa of Avila's The Way of Perfection came from a 1964 paperback whose added matter is still under copyright, in a 1946 translation by a British scholar whose US status we could not settle. A. W. Pink's Comfort for Christians, The Doctrine of Justification and Tithing were written after he moved back to Britain in 1934; if they were first printed there, their US copyright was restored in 1996, and we could not show otherwise. They leave the connector at its next update. Pink's earlier books and Teresa's other works stay.
  • Erasmus's date corrected again. John Wilson's translation of In Praise of Folly is from 1668, not 1688 as given above.
  • Reprint years were shown as publication years. Eight works carried the year of a modern reprint. Each now has the year the work, or the translation in the file, was first published:
    • Barnes's Notes on the New Testament: 1949 corrected to 1832.
    • Edersheim's Life and Times of Jesus the Messiah: 1953 corrected to 1883.
    • Lightfoot's Apostolic Fathers: 1956 corrected to 1891.
    • Thomson's Life of Dr Owen: 1965 corrected to 1850.
    • Torrey's Person and Work of the Holy Spirit: 1974 corrected to 1910.
    • Van Braght's Martyrs Mirror (Sohm's translation): 1950 corrected to 1886.
    • MacDonald's The Princess and Curdie: 1934 corrected to 1883.
    • Finney's Power From On High: 1944 corrected to 1871, when its chapters began to appear.
  • 459 works had no year and now have one. 204 Greek and Latin texts take the year of their print edition. 219 other works take the year printed in the book itself. 36 volumes of Spurgeon's sermons take the year of the volume. About 440 works still have no year.
  • Broken characters were repaired in 40 files. More than 2,400 dashes, quotation marks, accented letters and Greek letters had been damaged when the texts were copied, most of them in the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, ten volumes of Spurgeon's sermons and Hannah Whitall Smith's Old Testament Types and Teachings. Each was restored from the source. 179 could not be recovered and are left as they are, 170 of them in one diagram in the Treasury.
