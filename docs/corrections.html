Corrections log

Errors found and fixed in the books, the connector and the library's public pages, newest first.

To report one, write to hello@christianopenlibrary.org.

19 November 2026

The books

  • 1,222 book titles were cut off partway. The early English books were turned into text by a script that stopped short titles at 80 characters. Titles ended in the middle of a phrase. Bunyan’s 1678 Pilgrim’s Progress was listed as “The Pilgrim’s Progress from This World to That Which Is to”. Most of these books are from EEBO-TCP (1,199 of them). The other 23 came from other sources and were cut the same way. Each title now ends where the title ends, in the catalog, the connector, the website search and the heading at the top of each book. The text of the books is unchanged. In place from 19 November.

18 November 2026

The connector

  • Entries in Wilson’s 1661 Complete Christian Dictionary showed stray marks. Numbered senses began with “*;;”, as in “*;;2. The strength of the wicked”. The marks came from the way the book was typed in, not from the book. They are removed from the 1,696 entries that had them. In place from 18 November.
  • “[gap: foreign]” was hard to understand. The people who typed in the early English books left out words in Greek, Hebrew and other non-Latin alphabets. They marked each place. The connector and the website search now show the mark as “[non-Latin text not transcribed]”. The books themselves are unchanged. In place from 18 November.

17 November 2026

The connector

  • Psalm citations in the Latin and Greek editions were counted under the wrong psalm. These editions follow the Vulgate and Septuagint. They number most Psalms one lower than the KJV and count a psalm's heading as its first verse. Their "Psalm 118" is the KJV's 119, but it was counted as the KJV's 118. About 1,000 references were dropped because the verse did not exist in the wrong psalm. They are now converted to the KJV's psalm and verse; where the verse cannot be converted with confidence, only the psalm is counted. In these 578 works, Psalm citations go from about 3,200 to 4,200. Reported by a library user. In place from the rebuild on the night of 17 November.
  • Psalm citations in 47 Catholic works in English were counted under the wrong psalm, for the same reason. These books cite the Psalms by the Douay-Rheims numbers, which are the Vulgate's: Aquinas's Summa cites "Ps. 118:100" for the KJV's 119:100. We compared the words quoted beside each Psalm reference in every English work with the Douay-Rheims and KJV verses of that number. Where the quotations match the Douay-Rheims and almost never the KJV, the references are now converted like the Latin editions. These works include Aquinas's Summa and Summa Contra Gentiles, Liguori, Francis de Sales, Francis of Assisi, Anselm, Gregory's Pastoral Rule, Tauler, Challoner and others. In these works, Psalm citations go from about 1,700 to 2,200. Works that give both numbers are unchanged, like the Catholic Encyclopedia. So is Aquinas's Catena Aurea, which uses the KJV's numbers. In place from the rebuild on the night of 17 November.

16 November 2026

The connector

  • Another website could make the Browse page search the books. A link to the Browse page with words in it looks in the books by itself. Now it waits for the visitor to press Enter when it is inside another site’s frame or in a window another page opened. The search box on every page already did this. Another site can no longer spend its visitors’ daily searches. There were two smaller fixes on the same page. When the server says there have been too many searches, the page stops searching by itself until the wait is over (Enter still works). A book’s “Read at” link is now made only from a web address (security review). In place from 16 November.
  • The quotation checker was replaced by a library search. The checker’s verdicts and share pictures could mislead on famous misquotes, so it was taken off the website. In its place, a search box on every page shows the passages themselves. Each comes with the book, year, page and a link to the source. The reader does the judging. The checker’s address on the server is now switched off. A security review before release found two small issues on the new page and both were fixed (security review). In place from 16 November.
  • The quotation check gave some wrong or confusing answers. Between 14 and 16 November it was fixed in several ways. It now tells present-day writers from older ones with similar names. It knows other forms of a writer's name ("Augustine of Hippo", "M'Cheyne"). It finds reworded quotations and quotations of very common words. It no longer counts a catechism's "A." as a missing word. It says a word differs in a scanned book instead of saying "word for word". It reports pieces joined by "..." as words left out. It says when a search ran out of time instead of saying "not found". It is no longer on the website and remains a tool in AI apps.

The books

  • The Paradise or Garden of the Holy Fathers was credited to Athanasius, and had no text. Its two files held only links to page images. The work is E. A. Wallis Budge's 1907 translation of a seventh-century Syriac collection of Athanasius's Life of Antony, Palladius, Jerome and the Sayings of the Fathers. The empty files are removed and Budge's full text is added under his name. In place from the rebuild on the night of 16 November.

15 November 2026

The connector

  • Some Scripture citations were counted under the wrong book. A book number that does not exist was read as the first book. Examples are "3 Chron." (a scanning error for "2 Chron.") and "3 Cor.". The older Catholic names "4 Kings" and "IV Reg." were read as 1 Kings. Such numbers are now left out. "3 Kings", "4 Kings", "III Reg." and "IV Reg." now count as 1 and 2 Kings. "3 Esdras" and "4 Esdras" count as 1 and 2 Esdras. About 2,100 citations move to the right book and about 500 wrong ones are dropped. Reported by a library user. In place from the rebuild on the night of 15 November.

Public pages

  • The home page said there were no modern books. Present-day church documents are in the connector by permission of their church bodies. It now says so. The home and download pages also said the library had about 6,400 works in 15,300 files. It had about 7,100 works in 16,600 files.

14 November 2026

Public pages

  • The cost card planned for far more people than use it. From 12 November it costed the coming months as if hundreds of people a day used the connector. It didn't say so. December showed about $102. It now projects from actual use (December: about $42).
  • The security review's summary was out of date. It still said the connector only reads public-domain books and stores no accounts. It now says it also holds a few documents shown by permission of their church bodies, and that sign-in accounts are held by WorkOS. The reviews are now listed newest first.

13 November 2026

The books

  • Three works were removed or replaced for copyright reasons. One copy of Bunyan's Pilgrim's Progress was a 1988 edition with a modern introduction; Bunyan's text stays, from Project Gutenberg and EEBO-TCP. Pascal's Memorial came with an unsigned modern introduction and translation, and was removed. The Confession of St. Patrick named no translator; it is replaced by an 1880 translation from Project Gutenberg, with the Letter to Coroticus.
  • Church family records corrected. Several dates and other names were fixed, for example the Bulgarian Exarchate (1870 and 1872).

11 November 2026

Public pages

  • "Exactly as printed" was wrong. The front page said passages were returned exactly as printed. They are returned exactly as they stand in the library's copy. The copies have errors, most of all in scanned books. The page now says so.
  • Not every result has a year and page. The front page and the connector's description promised both. Some sources give neither. Both now say "where the source gives them".
  • "Stores no accounts" and "private by design" said too much. Sign-in accounts are held by WorkOS, and search words go to Voyage AI. The claims are replaced by a page naming everyone who sees a question.
  • The Voyage AI account is now opted out of data use. Until today it wasn't, so Voyage's terms allowed it to keep search words and use them to improve its models.
  • "No affiliation with any church or denomination" was reworded to what is meant: no church or denomination directs or endorses the project.
  • The security review is described more exactly. It was carried out with Claude, Anthropic's AI. It wasn't an outside audit.
  • Monergism's editions were removed. We asked Monergism for permission to keep 338 texts taken from its free ebooks. Monergism replied that its editions may not be placed in another library, database or AI system. All 338 were removed from the library and the connector. A separate collection of Monergism's ebooks that had been shared with library members is no longer shared. The same works will be added again from other sources.
  • Most of the removed works are back, from other sources. Of the 338 works, 126 were already in the library inside their authors' collected Works. Another 161 were added again from independent copies: EEBO-TCP, Project Gutenberg and Internet Archive scans. 51 are not back. 12 were Monergism's own compilations. For the other 39, no usable independent copy was found.
  • CCEL is now credited. CCEL confirmed that its texts may be quoted and used as long as its files are not sold.
  • Credit added for the Greek and Latin texts. They come from the Open Greek and Latin Project under CC BY-SA 4.0. Each file and the front page now give the credit, the license and a note of what was changed.

The connector

  • A missing book is now said plainly. Asking for a book the library doesn't have used to return loosely related titles with a small note at the end. The result now opens with "NOT FOUND" and tells the AI not to quote the book from memory.
  • Results now say how well they match. Each is marked exact, close, partial or loose. When nothing is close, the result opens with "NO CLOSE MATCH" or "NOT FOUND".
  • Scanned passages are now marked in search results. Before, only the book listing said a text was a scan.

The books

  • Berkhof's Systematic Theology was removed. It is a revised edition of his Reformed Dogmatics (1932). That copyright was renewed in 1960. Much of its text is still protected in the United States until 1 January 2028. Other sites list it as public domain; that doesn't hold. It is out of the library and leaves the connector at its next update. Berkhof's Summary of Christian Doctrine (1938, never renewed) and Introduction to the New Testament (1915) stay.
  • Two more reprint years were corrected. Erasmus's In Praise of Folly: 1958 corrected to 1668, the year of John Wilson's translation in the file. Melanchthon's Apology of the Augsburg Confession: 1989 corrected to 1921, the Bente and Dau translation in the Triglot Concordia.
  • Four more works were removed. Teresa of Avila's The Way of Perfection came from a 1964 paperback. Its added matter is still under copyright. The 1946 translation is by a British scholar whose US status we could not settle. A. W. Pink's Comfort for Christians, The Doctrine of Justification and Tithing were written after he moved back to Britain in 1934. If they were first printed there, their US copyright was restored in 1996. We could not show otherwise. They leave the connector at its next update. Pink's earlier books and Teresa's other works stay.
  • Reprint years were shown as publication years. Eight works carried the year of a modern reprint. Each now has the year the work or the translation in the file was first published:
    • Barnes's Notes on the New Testament: 1949 corrected to 1832.
    • Edersheim's Life and Times of Jesus the Messiah: 1953 corrected to 1883.
    • Lightfoot's Apostolic Fathers: 1956 corrected to 1891.
    • Thomson's Life of Dr Owen: 1965 corrected to 1850.
    • Torrey's Person and Work of the Holy Spirit: 1974 corrected to 1910.
    • Van Braght's Martyrs Mirror (Sohm's translation): 1950 corrected to 1886.
    • MacDonald's The Princess and Curdie: 1934 corrected to 1883.
    • Finney's Power From On High: 1944 corrected to 1871, when its chapters began to appear.
  • 459 works had no year and now have one. 204 Greek and Latin texts take the year of their print edition. 219 other works take the year printed in the book itself. 36 volumes of Spurgeon's sermons take the year of the volume. About 440 works still have no year.
  • Broken characters were repaired in 40 files. More than 2,400 dashes, quotation marks, accented letters and Greek letters had been damaged when the texts were copied. Most were in the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, ten volumes of Spurgeon's sermons and Hannah Whitall Smith's Old Testament Types and Teachings. Each was restored from the source. 179 could not be recovered and are left as they are. 170 of them are in one diagram in the Treasury.
