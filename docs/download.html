Download the books

  • 9,700 files
  • About 3 GB
  • Free GitHub account

The connector lets you search the library from your AI app. If you'd rather have the books themselves on your own computer, you can download a single book or the whole library.

What you get

  • About 7,100 works in 16,600 files. Each book is a Markdown file: plain text with simple formatting, which opens in any text editor and in note apps such as Obsidian.
  • About 3 GB for the whole library, once unzipped.
  • Long books are split into parts. Open the file without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.
  • Every file names its source and license at the top, so you can see which edition you are reading.

Getting access

The books are shared by invitation, through GitHub, so this part needs a free GitHub account. (The AI connector doesn't: anyone can set that up with an email address.)

  1. Make a free account at github.com.
  2. Ask for an invitation with the request form. The form is public, so leave out private details.
  3. Accept the invitation in the email from GitHub.

Download one book

  1. Look the book up in the catalog. Search for one distinctive word, such as Baxter, since old titles are spelled in many ways. Note the Folder column.
  2. Open that folder in the library and click the book.
  3. Choose Download raw file, at the top right.

Download the whole library

  1. Open the library.
  2. Choose Code › Download ZIP.
  3. Unzip it.

To get books added later, download it again. Or, to keep it up to date: install GitHub Desktop, choose Code › Open with GitHub Desktop on the library's page, and later choose Fetch origin, then Pull origin, to get new books.

Reading them in Obsidian

Drag the library folder into your vault, or open it as its own vault with Open folder as vault. For a long book, open the note without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.

What you may do with the books

You can use the books for yourself and in small free projects. You can't put them in apps, AI services or anything that makes money. We don't make exceptions, so please don't ask.

Why: the collection took years to gather and check, every AI search costs money, and some texts are here only by permission.

Yes, no need to ask:

  • Read, study, print and keep them.
  • Quote them in sermons, lessons, books, articles and posts, including books you sell. Give the source.
  • Give one book to someone.
  • Load them into your own AI tool, such as NotebookLM or a ChatGPT project, for your own study.
  • Use up to 10 books in a free project, such as a study guide, a church reading list or a class handout. That's 10 books in total, however many versions, volumes or spin-offs the project has; a book in several files counts as one. Keep the words unchanged (no AI rewrites or translations in their place; your own translation, marked as yours, is fine), credit the source named in each file with a link to christianopenlibrary.org, and don't suggest the library endorses it.

No:

Unless a book's own license allows it (see below).

  • Apps, websites or AI services built on the collection or a large part of it (more than 10 books).
  • Anything paid: anything sold, or with subscriptions, paid upgrades, ads or sponsorship (quoting, above, is the exception).
  • AI training or datasets, for any purpose, your own included.
  • Copies of the collection: mirrors, public uploads, shared drives, or passing on your download or sign-in. Each person, in a church or class too, needs their own.
  • The AI connector behind anything else, such as another app, website, bot or service, or to copy texts out in bulk.

Never:

  • Documents used by permission. A few present-day church documents are in the AI connector by their owners' permission. They are not in the downloads. Quote only what you need and credit the church body; never copy or share one whole.

Need more? Use the originals

The originals are free at their own sources. Every file names where it was copied from, such as Early English Books Online (EEBO-TCP), the Christian Classics Ethereal Library, Internet Archive or Project Gutenberg. Use them there, on those sites' own terms.

Each book's own license

These rules are the terms of membership, not a copyright claim. A book's license is on its license: line, at the top of the file without "Part" in its name.

  • CC BY and CC BY-SA books, such as the Greek and Latin texts from the Open Greek and Latin Project, may be shared as their license allows, whatever this page says. Credit the source named in the file, and share CC BY-SA texts, and your changes to them, under the same license.
  • Texts from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library (source on ccel.org) must never be sold.
  • The Intermediate Greek-English Lexicon (from Perseus) is for non-commercial use only: keep its license statement and offer any changes to Perseus.
  • Project Gutenberg texts keep Gutenberg's license at the top.

Breaking these rules ends your access. The library's copies are shared as they are, with no warranty.
