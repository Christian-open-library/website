Download the books

Rather have the books themselves? Download one or the whole library.

What you get

  • Over 7,600 works in about 17,600 files. Each book is a Markdown file. That's plain text with simple formatting. It opens in any text editor and in note apps such as Obsidian.
  • About 3 GB for the whole library once unzipped.
  • Long books are split into parts. Open the file without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.
  • Every file names its source and license at the top. You can see which edition you are reading.

Getting access

The books are shared by invitation through GitHub. You need a free GitHub account for this. (The AI connector doesn't.)

  1. Make a free account at github.com.
  2. Ask for an invitation with the request form. The form is public. Leave out private details.
  3. Accept the invitation in the email from GitHub.

Download one book

  1. Look the book up in the catalog. Search for one distinctive word like Baxter. Old titles are spelled in many ways. Note the Folder column.
  2. Open that folder in the library and click the book.
  3. Choose Download raw file at the top right.

Download the whole library

  1. Open the library.
  2. Choose Code › Download ZIP.
  3. Unzip it.

To get books added later, download it again. Or keep it up to date with GitHub Desktop. Install it and choose Code › Open with GitHub Desktop on the library's page. Later, choose Fetch origin and then Pull origin to get new books.

Reading them in Obsidian

Drag the library folder into your vault. Or open it as its own vault with Open folder as vault. For a long book, open the note without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.

What you may do with the books

You can use the books for yourself and in small free projects. You can't put them in apps, AI services or anything that makes money without a written license from us.

Why? The collection took years to gather and check. Every AI search costs money. Some texts are here only by permission.

Yes, no need to ask:

  • Read, study, print and keep them.
  • Quote them in sermons, lessons, books, articles and posts. That includes books you sell. Give the source.
  • Give one book to someone.
  • Load them into your own AI tool for your own study, like NotebookLM or a ChatGPT project.
  • Use up to 10 books in a free project like a study guide, a church reading list or a class handout. That's 10 books in total, however many versions, volumes or spin-offs the project has. A book in several files counts as one. A collected Works set is many books: each treatise in it counts as its own book. Keep the words unchanged. No AI rewrites or translations in their place. Your own translation is fine if it's marked as yours. Credit the source named in each file with a link to christianopenlibrary.org. Don't suggest the library endorses it.

No:

Unless a book's own license allows it (see below).

  • Apps, websites or AI services built on the collection or a large part of it (more than 10 books) without a written license from us.
  • Anything paid without a written license from us. That means anything sold or with subscriptions, paid upgrades, ads or sponsorship. Quoting is the exception (see above).
  • AI training or datasets for any purpose, your own included. No license covers this.
  • Copies of the collection: mirrors, public uploads and shared drives. Don't pass on your download or sign-in. Each person needs their own, in a church or class too.
  • The AI connector behind anything else, like another app, website, bot or service. Or using it to copy texts out in bulk.

Never:

  • Documents used by permission. Some present-day church documents are in the AI connector by their owners' permission. They are not in the downloads. Quote only what you need and credit the church body. Never copy or share one whole.

Need more? Use the originals

The originals are free at their own sources. Every file names where it was copied from. That might be Early English Books Online (EEBO-TCP), the Christian Classics Ethereal Library, Internet Archive or Project Gutenberg. Use them there on those sites' own terms.

Free projects stop at 10 books. For more or for anything paid, write to hello@christianopenlibrary.org. Licenses are agreed case by case and never include texts from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library, the Perseus lexicon or documents used by permission.

Each book's own license

These rules are the terms of membership, not a copyright claim. A book's license is on its license: line. It's at the top of the file without "Part" in its name.

  • CC BY and CC BY-SA books may be shared as their license allows, whatever this page says. The Greek and Latin texts from the Open Greek and Latin Project are like this. Credit the source named in the file. Share CC BY-SA texts and your changes to them under the same license.
  • Texts from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library (source on ccel.org) must never be sold.
  • The Intermediate Greek-English Lexicon (from Perseus) is for non-commercial use only: keep its license statement and offer any changes to Perseus.
  • Project Gutenberg texts keep Gutenberg's license at the top.

Breaking these rules ends your access. The library's copies are shared as they are, with no warranty.
