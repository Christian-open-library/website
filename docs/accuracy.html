How well it finds things

The connector is tested on a fixed set of questions with known answers. Here are the results, misses included.

Tested 11 November 2026, after the search upgrade described below.

Finding the right book

Each of 75 questions has a known right answer: a work the passage must come from. The test asks the live connector each question and checks whether a passage from that work is among the first 3 and the first 8 results.

Did the right book appear in the results?
Kind of questionQuestionsIn the first 3In the first 8
A remembered quotation191819
A paraphrase from memory161212

All7560 (80%)65 (87%)
  • It is strongest on quotations. If you remember the words, it nearly always finds them.
  • It is weakest on paraphrases. If you remember only the gist, it finds the passage about three times in four. Try two or three phrasings, or one distinctive phrase.
  • A search upgrade helped, but not everywhere. Earlier that day, before searches were understood by a larger Voyage AI model, the same test scored 53 of 75 in the first 3 and 61 of 75 in the first 8. Paraphrases improved most (9 of 16 became 12 of 16 in the first 8). Doctrine questions slipped from 10 to 9 of 13, which may be because the library also grew by about 130 books that day.
  • A miss doesn't always mean a wrong answer. On doctrine questions several works say much the same thing, and the test counts only the one named in advance.

Saying when it has nothing

These checks were run before that day's search upgrade and have not been repeated yet.

Each result is marked as an exact, close, partial or loose match. When nothing is close, the result opens with "NO CLOSE MATCH". When everything is loose, it opens with "NOT FOUND". The cut-offs were set from the searches below, which were then run against the live connector.

Does it say so when it has nothing?
TestSearchesOutcome
Subjects the library has nothing about (a home server, a sourdough starter, a salary cap)10All 10 came back "NOT FOUND".
Modern authors the library doesn't have (Barth, Lewis, Keller, Packer and others)108 came back "NO CLOSE MATCH". 2 came back with a close match, because older writers in the library say something very similar.
The 75 questions above756 came back "NO CLOSE MATCH". In 4 of those the right work really was missing from the results. In the other 2 it was there.

In this test "NOT FOUND" was never wrong. A "close" match means the passage is about what was asked. It doesn't mean it is by the author you named, so check the author and title on every result.

How it searches

Each search runs two ways at once. A keyword search finds passages that contain your words, and it matches old spellings automatically. A meaning search finds passages about what you described. The meaning search uses embedding models from Voyage AI: the books are indexed with voyage-4-lite, and your question is understood by the larger voyage-4-large. A third Voyage model, rerank-3, then puts the combined results in order.

To do this, Voyage sees your search words and the passages it is ranking, and keeps nothing (see Privacy). The scores on this page were measured on exactly this setup. The code is public.

What isn't measured

  • The AI app's answer. These tests cover what the connector returns. Your AI app then writes the answer, and it can summarize badly or quote from memory. Nothing here measures that.
  • Errors in the texts. Scanned books have misread letters. The error rate hasn't been measured yet.
  • Whether the best passage came first. The test counts a passage from the right work. It doesn't judge whether it is the best passage in that work.

The questions are public

The 75 questions, the 20 searches the library can't answer (unanswerable.jsonl) and the test itself are in the connector's code: tests/eval. When someone reports a missed book or a bad quotation, it becomes a new question in the set.
