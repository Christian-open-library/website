What's in the library

  • Theology and devotion

    The Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Edwards, and the historic creeds and confessions.

  • Church records

    Constitutions, disciplines, digests and minutes of more than 80 denominations, up to 1930.

  • Commentaries

    Calvin, Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, and Keil and Delitzsch. Also Greek and Hebrew lexicons.

  • Sermons

    Spurgeon, Whitefield, M'Cheyne, Chalmers, and Simeon's 2,500 sermon outlines.

  • Journals and reference

    The Princeton Review (1831–1929), Bible translations and classic Bible dictionaries.

  • Poetry, fiction and philosophy

    Herbert, Milton, Donne, George MacDonald, Chesterton, Plato, Aristotle and others.

Every work is public domain or openly licensed, apart from church documents shared by their owners' permission. There is no AI-written or AI-translated text: every book is the published work of a human author or translator. Browse the full list.

Ask about your own church

Tell your AI app which church you belong to. It will search only that church's records and those of the bodies it came from.

Questions you can ask

  • What did Matthew Henry say about Romans 8:28?
  • Find where a Puritan writes about assurance of salvation.
  • Which older writers quoted Isaiah 53?
  • How did the Methodist Discipline handle church trials in 1884?

It searches by meaning as well as exact words. You can describe a passage you only half remember.

Support the library

The library is free for everyone and has no ads. Gifts pay for the server and the search service. They also pay for the work of finding, checking and adding books.

About

The Christian Open Library is an independent library of Christian books and church records. Matt Miller, a pastor in the Presbyterian Church in America, founded it. It is not a ministry of any church, presbytery or denomination. None of them directs or endorses it. Write to us at hello@christianopenlibrary.org.

Credits and licences

Many texts come from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library at Calvin University. It asks that its files not be sold. The books here are free to everyone and are never sold. Others come from the Text Creation Partnership (EEBO-TCP, Evans-TCP and ECCO-TCP), the Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks. Every book's file names the page it came from.

The Greek and Latin texts of the Church Fathers and Philo come from the Open Greek and Latin Project at the University of Leipzig. So does Yonge's English translation of Philo. They are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. The library converted them from TEI XML to Markdown and shares its copies under the same license.
