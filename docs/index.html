Search thousands of old Christian books by asking a question

The Christian Open Library is about 4,600 public-domain books in plain text: the Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Spurgeon, commentaries, creeds and more. You connect it to your AI app (Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini), and the app can search the books and quote them word for word, with the author and title.

It is free. You do not need a GitHub account or any technical skill.

Set it up (about five minutes)

What you can ask

Ask in your own words, as you would ask a well-read friend:

  • What did Matthew Henry say about Romans 8:28?
  • Find where a Puritan writes about assurance of salvation.
  • Which older writers quoted Isaiah 53?
  • What did Spurgeon preach on Psalm 23?
  • What does the Westminster Confession say about the Sabbath?
  • Where does Calvin discuss the Lord's Supper?

It searches by meaning, not only by exact words, so you can describe what you half remember. It also holds seven Bible translations, Bible dictionaries, and Greek and Hebrew study helps.

What is in the library

  • Theology and devotion: the Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Edwards, and the historic creeds and confessions.
  • Commentaries and original-language help: Calvin, Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, Keil and Delitzsch, the Expositor's Greek Testament, Greek and Hebrew lexicons and grammars.
  • Sermons: Spurgeon, Whitefield, M'Cheyne, Chalmers, and Simeon's 2,500 sermon outlines.
  • Journals: the Princeton Review (1831–1929), with Charles Hodge, the Alexanders, Warfield and Machen.
  • Poetry, fiction and philosophy: Herbert, Milton, Donne, George MacDonald, Chesterton, Plato, Aristotle, Kant and more, in older translations.
  • Reference: seven Bibles, classic Bible dictionaries, and The Fundamentals.

Only public-domain and openly licensed works are included. The library does not have modern books.
