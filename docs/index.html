Christian Open Library

  • About 4,800 works
  • 80+ denominations
  • Public domain
  • Open code

A free library of about 4,800 public-domain Christian books and the historic records of more than 80 denominations. Ask a question in Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok, and get the passage quoted with its source.

What's in the library

  • Theology and devotion

    The Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Edwards, and the historic creeds and confessions.

  • Church records

    Constitutions, disciplines, digests and minutes of more than 80 denominations, up to 1930.

  • Commentaries

    Calvin, Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, Keil and Delitzsch, with Greek and Hebrew lexicons.

  • Sermons

    Spurgeon, Whitefield, M'Cheyne, Chalmers, and Simeon's 2,500 sermon outlines.

  • Journals and reference

    The Princeton Review (1831–1929), Bible translations and classic Bible dictionaries.

  • Poetry, fiction and philosophy

    Herbert, Milton, Donne, George MacDonald, Chesterton, Plato, Aristotle and others.

Only public-domain and openly licensed works are included, so there are no modern books. There is no AI-written or AI-translated text: every book is the published work of a human author or translator. Browse the full list.

Ask about your own church

Tell your AI app which church you belong to, and it searches only the records of that church and the bodies it came from.

“I'm in the PCA. What did our Book of Church Order say about the qualifications of ruling elders?”

These are historical editions, published before 1931. Your church's current rules may differ, and some of these bodies have since merged or divided. Current editions are added only where a denomination gives permission.

Questions you can ask

  • What did Matthew Henry say about Romans 8:28?
  • Find where a Puritan writes about assurance of salvation.
  • Which older writers quoted Isaiah 53?
  • How did the Methodist Discipline handle church trials in 1884?

It searches by meaning as well as by exact words, so you can describe a passage you only half remember.

Keep it running

The library is free, with no ads. It costs a little each month to run. If it's useful to you, you can give by card, GitHub Sponsors or Bitcoin.

Credits and licences

Many texts come from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library at Calvin University, which asks that its files not be sold. This library is free and sells nothing. Others come from the Text Creation Partnership (EEBO-TCP, Evans-TCP and ECCO-TCP), the Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks. Every book's file names the page it came from.

The Greek and Latin texts of the Church Fathers and Philo, and Yonge's English translation of Philo, come from the Open Greek and Latin Project at the University of Leipzig, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. They were converted from TEI XML to Markdown, and the library's copies are shared under the same license.
