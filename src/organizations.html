ECO: sacraments

ECO: A Covenant Order of Evangelical Presbyterians. Its Constitution and Confessional Standards are used by permission.

Can a ruling elder administer the sacraments in ECO, and what did the older Presbyterian church say about that?

Searched the Christian Open Library

ECO's Constitution. A ruling elder can administer the sacraments only when commissioned for it. Section 2.0503 (p. 26) says “The presbytery, in consultation with the session or other responsible committee, may authorize an elder or deacon to be commissioned to serve in the role of pastor/head of staff of a local congregation or a church plant. With approval of the presbytery, these individuals could moderate session and congregational meetings, administer the sacraments…” The commission lasts “twelve months and may be renewed indefinitely.” Just before that (pp. 25–26), an officer commissioned by the session to lead satellite congregations or house churches, and “appropriately trained, … shall be authorized to administer the sacraments in these communities.” Official copy: eco-pres.org

Older Presbyterian records (historical; ECO's rules today may differ). The PCUSA's 1926 Manual for Church Officers and Members quotes the Confession: neither sacrament “may be dispensed by any, but by a minister of the Word, lawfully ordained.” It concludes: “Under the explicit terms of the Constitution ruling elders cannot administer sealing ordinances.” Ruling elders could help pass the elements. Scan: archive.org

The PCUSA Digest (1923, Vol. 1, p. 47) records the 1906 General Assembly's response to the Presbytery of Winnebago's request to authorize ruling elders to administer the sacraments “when necessary”: “It is recommended that the request be not granted.” Scan: archive.org

Not an official ruling of ECO. Check the official copy.

ECO: property

ECO: A Covenant Order of Evangelical Presbyterians. Its Constitution and Confessional Standards are used by permission.

Does ECO hold a congregation's property in trust for the denomination, the way the old Presbyterian Church did?

Searched the Christian Open Library

ECO's Constitution (2026, §4.01, pp. 33–34). The Constitution has no denominational trust clause. Each congregation forms its own nonprofit corporation, which has the power to “Receive, hold, encumber, manage, and transfer property, real or personal” and to “hold and defend title to such property” (§4.0101). Buying, selling or mortgaging real property needs “the approval of the congregation, granted at a duly constituted meeting.” The presbytery “shall not... direct the use of, co-sign, or guarantee any debt instrument issued by a member congregation” (§4.0102). Official copy: eco-pres.org

Older Presbyterian records. These are historical and may differ from ECO's rules today.

  • PCUSA Manual for Church Officers and Members (1926): “All church property is a trust... the property is in law a trust, and the civil courts will protect that trust.” Find on scan
  • UPCNA Digest (1892): aided congregations had to hold property “in trust as and for a place of divine worship, subject to the standards and acts of the United Presbyterian Church of North America.” Find on scan

So, going by the Constitution's text, the congregation's own corporation holds title in ECO. The older bodies treated congregational property as held in trust for the church.

Not an official ruling of ECO. Check the official copy.

ECO: a 2025 Synod

ECO: A Covenant Order of Evangelical Presbyterians. Its Constitution and Confessional Standards are used by permission.

What did ECO's 2025 Synod decide about changes to the Rules of Discipline?

Searched the Christian Open Library

The library doesn't have a record of what ECO's 2025 Synod decided about the Rules of Discipline. It holds ECO's Constitution (2026 edition) and Presbyterian records from before 1931. It has no 2025 Synod minutes or actions.

What ECO's Constitution says about changing the Rules of Discipline. Section 6.02 covers amendments. It says “The Constitution of ECO includes the Essential Tenets, the Polity, and the Rules of Discipline.” It also says “Presbyteries may overture the synod of ECO for changes in this Constitution,” and that “Two presbyteries must concur in the proposed changes.” The synod may amend the changes and send them to the presbyteries. “A two-thirds affirmative vote of the presbyteries shall be required for adoption.” (ECO, Constitution (2026), §6.02, p. 36. Used by permission. Official copy: eco-pres.org)

Older Presbyterian records. These are historical and may differ from ECO's rules today. The PCUS Assembly sent amendments to the Rules of Discipline to the presbyteries. One overture drew a vote of “60 approving and 3 disapproving,” and the Assembly then enacted the additions. (PCUS, Nicolassen, Digest of the Acts and Proceedings of the General Assembly (1923), p. 91. Scan page: archive.org)

For the 2025 Synod's action, check ECO's official Synod minutes.

Not an official ruling of ECO. Check the official copy.

PCA: ruling elders

Presbyterian Church in America. Answered from the records of the churches it comes from, all before 1931.

What did our Book of Church Order say about the qualifications of ruling elders?

Searched the Christian Open Library

The Presbyterian Church in the U.S. (Southern Presbyterian) Book of Church Order, 1925 edition, p. 19, says of ruling elders:

“Those who fill this office ought to be blameless in life and sound in the faith; they should be men of wisdom and discretion; and by the holiness of their walk and conversation should be examples to the flock.”

(Scan page)

The same wording is in the PCUS 1900 edition, p. 14, and the 1881 edition. The 1925 edition adds that ruling elders “should, moreover, cultivate zealously their aptness to teach the Bible.”

The PCUS General Assembly digest (Alexander, 1888, p. 38) declined to require an examination on the church's standards before ordination. It said the ordination vows “cannot be conscientiously taken without previous careful examination of and intelligent acquaintance with the doctrinal standards of the Church.” (Scan page)

These are historical editions, and the PCA's current rules may differ.

Not an official ruling. Check the current Book of Church Order.
