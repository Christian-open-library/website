Christian Open Library

  • About 4,800 works
  • 80+ denominations
  • Public domain
  • Open code

A free collection of about 4,800 public-domain Christian books and the historic records of more than 80 denominations, which you can search from Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok. Ask a question in your own words, and your AI app finds the passages in the library and quotes them with the author and title.

How it works

  1. Copy the address below.
  2. Add it to your AI app's settings (instructions for Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok).
  3. Sign in with Google, or with your email address (you'll get a short code). No GitHub account needed.
  4. Ask a question.
https://mcp.christianopenlibrary.org/mcp

Questions you can ask

  • What did Matthew Henry say about Romans 8:28?
  • Find where a Puritan writes about assurance of salvation.
  • Which older writers quoted Isaiah 53?
  • What did Spurgeon preach on Psalm 23?
  • What does the Westminster Confession say about the Sabbath?
  • What did the Southern Baptist Convention adopt as the Baptist Faith and Message in 1925?
  • How did the Methodist Discipline handle church trials in 1884?
  • What did the Presbyterian General Assembly of 1910 declare essential doctrines?

It searches by meaning as well as by exact words, so you can describe a passage you only half remember. It also has Bible translations, Bible dictionaries, and Greek and Hebrew study helps.

Ask about your denomination

Ask polity, history and theology questions about your own church. The library holds constitutions, books of order and discipline, digests of decisions, and General Assembly, convention and conference minutes from more than 80 church bodies: Presbyterian, Baptist, Methodist, Holiness, Anglican, Lutheran, Catholic, Orthodox, Quaker, Mennonite, Moravian and more. You can ask what your church's rules said in 1880, what an assembly decided in a controversy, or how a rule changed over the years.

Ask within your denomination. Tell your AI app which church you belong to, for example: "I'm in the PCA. What did our church's Book of Church Order say about the qualifications of ruling elders?" It then searches only the records of your church and the bodies it came from (for the PCA, the old Southern Presbyterian Church and, before 1861, the Presbyterian Church in the U.S.A.), and labels each answer with the body, the edition and its year. Present-day names and abbreviations work: PCA, OPC, PC(USA), EPC, ARP, SBC, UMC, LCMS, ELCA, Episcopal, ACNA, Catholic and more.

These are historical editions, published before 1931. Your church's current rules may differ, and some of these bodies have since merged or divided. Current editions are added only where a denomination gives permission.

What's in the library

  • Theology and devotion: the Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Edwards, and the historic creeds and confessions.
  • Church records: constitutions, disciplines, canons, digests and minutes of more than 80 denominations, up to 1930.
  • Commentaries: Calvin, Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, Keil and Delitzsch, the Expositor's Greek Testament, and Greek and Hebrew lexicons.
  • Sermons: Spurgeon, Whitefield, M'Cheyne, Chalmers, and Simeon's 2,500 sermon outlines.
  • Journals: the Princeton Review (1831–1929), with Charles Hodge, the Alexanders, Warfield and Machen.
  • Poetry, fiction and philosophy: Herbert, Milton, Donne, George MacDonald, Chesterton, Plato, Aristotle, Kant and others.
  • Reference: Bible translations, classic Bible dictionaries, and The Fundamentals.

Only public-domain and openly licensed works are included, so there are no modern books. Browse the full list.

Join the library on GitHub

The books themselves are shared on GitHub, as plain text files you can keep: github.com/Christian-open-library. To join, make a free GitHub account and ask for an invitation. Then you can download one book or all of them.

Checking the answers

Your AI app writes the answer, and it can summarize badly or quote from memory. So the library gives every passage with its source, and says so when it has nothing. Quotations from scanned books link straight to the printed page, with the words highlighted, and you can ask your AI app to check any quotation against the library. Before you print or use a quotation, check it. You can also see how well it finds things.

Who runs it

I'm Matt Miller, a pastor in the Presbyterian Church in America. The library is my personal project. It is not a ministry of my church, my presbytery or the PCA, and none of them directs or endorses it. It has no ads and no trackers, and nothing is sold.

Write to me at hello@christianopenlibrary.org if it missed a book, quoted something wrongly, or has a book that shouldn't be there.
