How it works

Three steps, about five minutes.

  1. i

    Paste one address

    In your AI app's settings, under connectors. There are instructions for Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini.

  2. ii

    Sign in with your email

    You'll get a short code to type in. No GitHub account, no password to remember.

  3. iii

    Ask a question

    In your own words, as you would ask a well-read friend. Your app searches the library for you.

Try asking

Ask in your own words.

It searches by meaning, not only by exact words, so you can describe what you half remember. It also holds seven Bible translations, Bible dictionaries, and Greek and Hebrew study helps.

The collection

Two thousand years of Christian writing.

Only public-domain and openly licensed works, from antiquity to the 1920s, in their original editions where possible.

  • Theology and devotion

    The Church Fathers, the Reformers, the Puritans, Edwards, and the historic creeds and confessions.

  • Commentaries

    Calvin, Matthew Henry, Gill, Poole, Keil and Delitzsch, the Expositor's Greek Testament, Greek and Hebrew lexicons.

  • Sermons

    Spurgeon, Whitefield, M'Cheyne, Chalmers, and Simeon's 2,500 sermon outlines on the whole Bible.

  • Journals

    The Princeton Review (1831–1929): Charles Hodge, the Alexanders, Warfield and Machen.

  • Poetry, fiction, philosophy

    Herbert, Milton, Donne, George MacDonald, Chesterton, Plato, Aristotle, Kant and more.

  • Reference

    Seven Bibles, classic Bible dictionaries, and The Fundamentals.

Check it, don't just believe it

Built so you can tell when it's wrong.

An AI app can summarize badly or quote from memory. The library gives you what you need to check every answer before you print or preach it.

  • Word for word

    Passages come exactly as they stand in the library's copy, with author and title, and the year and page where the source gives them.

  • Traceable

    Every result carries a reference and a link to where the library's copy came from.

  • Honest about gaps

    When the library has nothing, it says NOT FOUND and tells the AI not to fill the gap from memory.

  • Measured

    Tested on 75 questions with known answers. The results are published, misses included.

Credits

Many texts come from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library at Calvin University, which asks that its files not be sold. This library is free and sells nothing. Others come from the Text Creation Partnership (EEBO-TCP, Evans-TCP and ECCO-TCP), the Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks. Every book's file names the page it came from.

The Greek and Latin texts of the Church Fathers and Philo, and Yonge's English translation of Philo, come from the Open Greek and Latin Project at the University of Leipzig, licensed CC BY-SA 4.0. They were converted from TEI XML to Markdown, and the library's copies are shared under the same license.
