Download the books

  • 9,700 files
  • About 3 GB
  • Free GitHub account

The connector lets you search the library from your AI app. If you'd rather have the books themselves on your own computer, you can download a single book or the whole library.

What you get

  • About 4,800 works in 10,400 files. Each book is a Markdown file: plain text with simple formatting, which opens in any text editor and in note apps such as Obsidian.
  • About 3 GB for the whole library, once unzipped.
  • Long books are split into parts. Open the file without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.
  • Every file names its source and license at the top, so you can see which edition you are reading.

Getting access

The books are shared by invitation, through GitHub, so this part needs a free GitHub account. (The AI connector doesn't: anyone can set that up with an email address.)

  1. Make a free account at github.com.
  2. Ask for an invitation with the request form. The form is public, so leave out private details.
  3. Accept the invitation in the email from GitHub.

Download one book

  1. Look the book up in the catalog. Search for one distinctive word, such as Baxter, since old titles are spelled in many ways. Note the Folder column.
  2. Open that folder in the library and click the book.
  3. Choose Download raw file, at the top right.

Download the whole library

  1. Open the library.
  2. Choose Code › Download ZIP.
  3. Unzip it.

To get books added later, download it again. Or, to keep it up to date: install GitHub Desktop, choose Code › Open with GitHub Desktop on the library's page, and later choose Fetch origin, then Pull origin, to get new books.

Reading them in Obsidian

Drag the library folder into your vault, or open it as its own vault with Open folder as vault. For a long book, open the note without "Part" in its name: it links to each part.

What you may do with them

Members may read, quote, print and share the books for study, teaching, preaching and their own writing, and may give a single book to someone else.

Please ask first (hello@christianopenlibrary.org) before you:

  • republish the collection or a large part of it, for example as another library, website or download;
  • put it in an app, an AI service or anything you sell.

Some books carry their own terms as well. Each book's license is on its license: line, at the top of the file without "Part" in its name.

  • The Greek and Latin texts from the Open Greek and Latin Project, and other CC BY and CC BY-SA books, may be shared under their license. Credit the source named in the file, and share CC BY-SA texts, and your changes to them, under the same license.
  • Texts from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library (source on ccel.org) must never be sold, so never put them in anything you charge for.
  • Project Gutenberg texts keep Gutenberg's license at the top.
  • Don't build an app on the AI connector. The connector is for people asking questions, and each search costs money.

Breaking these terms ends your access. The library's copies are shared as they are, with no warranty.
