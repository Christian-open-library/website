Give your AI app the books themselves. Connect the library to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok. It then searches all 6,400 works and quotes the passage with its source, not from memory. Free.

Set it up

Why check a quotation here

  1. Quotations are often wrong. AI assistants asked for a quotation frequently reword it, credit it to the wrong author, or invent it. Quotation websites and social media repeat the same errors.
  2. This search reads the books. It compares your words with the text of 6,400 works, word by word. Nothing in the result is written by AI.
  3. Every result can be verified. It names the book, the year and the page, and links to a scan of the printed page where the library has one.
How to read the results
  • Found in the Bible. The words are a Bible verse, word for word in one of the library's seven translations (the King James Version first). The result gives the reference, then the works that quote it.
  • Verified word for word. The words are in the work named, as submitted. Spelling, capitals and punctuation are not compared.
  • Found with different wording. The passage is in the work, but the quotation has changed in circulation. The result shows the wording in the source.
  • Not found in the author's works. The words are in the library, but in none of its works by the author named. The result gives the earliest work that has them.
  • Not found in the library. No work in the library has the words or a close match.
Limits of this search
  • “Not found” does not mean false. The library holds about 6,400 works, not everything ever written. Most books published after 1930, such as those of C. S. Lewis, are still in copyright and are not included.
  • The earliest work in the library is not always the origin. A saying can be older than any book the library holds.
  • Scanned texts contain misread letters. Where a text was read by computer from a scan, compare the printed page before quoting it.

Privacy

The words you check go to the library's server and to Voyage AI, whose search index helps locate reworded passages. Cloudflare carries them. Neither the server nor Voyage keeps them. A share link carries the quotation inside the link itself, after the #, so this website never receives it. Your IP address is used only to count checks for the daily limit, as a scrambled code that changes every day and is deleted the next day. Details.
