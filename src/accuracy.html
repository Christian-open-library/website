Accuracy

Every quotation comes with a link to its source. Check it before you use it. This page shows how, what the library promises and how well it finds things.

How to check a quotation

  1. Find the link that comes with the quotation. Your AI app is told to put one after every quotation.
  2. Open it. A Scan page or Find on scan link opens the scanned book at the passage. The words are highlighted on the printed page. Other links go to where the library's copy came from.
  3. No link? Ask your AI app: "Give me the source link for that quotation." If it can't, don't use the quotation.
  4. Not sure who said it? Ask: "Check this quote: ...". The library's quote checker tells you if the words are in the library word for word or worded differently. It also tells you if someone else wrote them or if they aren't found. It says when a scanned book differs from your wording by a single letter (check the scan). It also says when the words are there only with others left out in between.

What the library promises

  • Quotations are word for word from the library's copy. Each comes with the author and title. The year and page are given where the source has them.
  • Every quotation can be traced. Each result has a reference and a link to where the library's copy came from. Results from scanned books link to the scan itself, open at the passage with the words highlighted.
  • It says when it has nothing. When the library doesn't have a book or has nothing close, the connector says so. It tells the AI not to fill the gap from memory. Each result is marked as an exact, close, partial or loose match.
  • Scans are marked. Every passage from a scanned book is marked as uncorrected OCR. That's a computer's reading of the page. It may have wrong letters.
  • Every book names its source and its license. Every work is public domain or openly licensed, apart from church documents shared by their owners' permission.
  • Texts aren't modernized. The library doesn't modernize, correct or abridge what it copies. Some sources had already edited their editions. Each file names its source, so you can see which edition you're reading.

What it can't promise

  • The copies aren't perfect. Scanned books have misread letters. Some texts have no year or page numbers.
  • Most church documents are historical. Most denominational records are editions from before 1931. A church's current rules may differ. Some bodies have since merged or divided.
  • Your AI app writes the answer. The connector finds and quotes. Your app (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Grok) reads the passages and writes the answer. It can summarize badly or quote from memory.

Finding the right book

Tested 12 November 2026 on the live connector.

We asked the library 75 questions where we already knew which book had the answer. It found the right book 70 times.

How we count: when you ask your AI app a question, the library hands it 8 passages to read. If one of them comes from the right book, that counts as found. In 64 of the 75, it was one of the first three.

  • It is strongest on quotations. If you remember the words, it nearly always finds them.
  • It is weakest on paraphrases. If you remember only the gist, it finds the passage about seven times in eight. Try two or three phrasings, or one distinctive phrase.
  • Part of the gain since 11 November is a fairer answer key. On 11 November it found the right book 65 times out of 75. Two "misses" were Brooks's Precious Remedies and Owen's Mortification of Sin. They were found inside their authors' collected works, which hold the same text. Those volumes now count. The rest of the change came from search and library changes. Doctrine questions were not re-graded: when another sound writer answers, it still counts as a miss.
  • The 5 misses. Two were doctrine questions where other works say much the same. Three were "who said this?" questions. For those it found the right subject but the wrong source. It gave a sermon retelling Augustine's conversion instead of his Confessions. It gave Puritans on sorrow for sin instead of Thomas à Kempis. And it gave other Spurgeon volumes instead of the sermon asked for.
  • A search upgrade helped. Searches are now read by a larger Voyage AI model. Before that, on 11 November, it found the right book 61 times out of 75.
  • A miss doesn't always mean a wrong answer. On doctrine questions several works say much the same thing, and the test counts only the one named in advance.

Saying when it has nothing

Tested again 12 November 2026, on the current connector.

Each result is marked as an exact, close, partial or loose match. When nothing is close, the result opens with "NO CLOSE MATCH". When everything is loose, it opens with "NOT FOUND". The cut-offs were set from the searches below, which were then run against the live connector.

The first run was on 11 November, before the search upgrade. It gave the same results for the first two tests. On the 75 questions it gave 6 warnings. In 4 of them the right work really was missing. A run earlier on 12 November gave 1. So a missing warning doesn't prove the right book was found.

In every run "NOT FOUND" was never wrong. A "close" match means the passage is about what was asked, not that it is the source. The connector now tells the AI so. It also tells it to use the quote checker for "who said this?" questions. Sometimes a close passage credits the words to another writer, as in "the story of Augustine" or "so Richard Baxter wrote". Then the result says it may be quoting or retelling that writer. On these 95 searches that note appeared twice, both times correctly. It can't catch a passage that is simply on the same subject. Still check the author and title on every result.

How it searches

Each search runs two ways at once. A keyword search finds passages that contain your words. It matches old spellings automatically. A meaning search finds passages about what you described. The meaning search uses embedding models from Voyage AI. The books are indexed with voyage-4-lite. Your question is read by the larger voyage-4-large. A third Voyage model, rerank-3, puts the combined results in order.

To do this, Voyage sees your search words and the passages it is ranking. It keeps nothing (see Privacy). The scores on this page were measured on exactly this setup. The code is public.

What isn't measured

  • The AI app's answer. These tests cover what the connector returns. Your AI app then writes the answer. It can summarize badly or quote from memory. Nothing here measures that.
  • Errors in the texts. Scanned books have misread letters. The error rate hasn't been measured yet.
  • Whether the best passage came first. The test counts a passage from the right work. It doesn't judge whether it is the best passage in that work.

The questions are public

The 75 questions, the 20 searches the library can't answer (unanswerable.jsonl) and the test itself are in the connector's code: tests/eval. When someone reports a missed book or a bad quotation, it becomes a new question in the set.
